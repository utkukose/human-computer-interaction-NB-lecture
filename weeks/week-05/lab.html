Heuristics and error lab

Three parts of this interactive lab are games in three dimensions (3D) that record your own actions. Part A asks you to inspect a kiosk screen with the ten heuristics. Part B repeats the discovery curve step by step. Part C puts you at an infusion pump with two modes, and Part D in a medication room with drugs whose names look alike. Part E asks for the values of the evaluation of the clinic app by hand. In the calculators every intermediate value is rounded, and the next step continues with the rounded value. The results of Parts A, C and D can be copied into the Colab notebook. Each game first shows its room of the hospital. Start moves the view to the screen, the pump or the cabinet, and the view returns to the room at the end. The games work best with a mouse or a touchpad on a computer.

Part A: Heuristic hunt at the kiosk

The check-in kiosk below shows the booking screen of a new supplier. The screen has ten usability problems, one for each of the ten heuristics of the lecture, among elements that work well. Evaluate the screen like an inspector: Click every element that breaks a heuristic. Each find shows the heuristic that it breaks and asks how severe the problem is, on the scale of 0 to 4 of the lecture.

The evaluation lasts four minutes, and the button below the scene ends it earlier. A click on an element without a problem counts as a false alarm. At the end, dashed boxes mark the problems that you missed, and the table lists all ten.

Part C: The infusion pump

The game below takes place in the ward again. The infusion pump on the drip stand of bed 1 has two modes. In the mode rate it reads a number as millilitres per hour, and in the mode volume to be infused it reads the same number as millilitres in total. The pump keeps the mode of its previous user, so it starts each order in either mode.

Each order asks for a rate. Check the mode, switch it with the key Mode if needed, type the number and press Start. Work as fast as a nurse on a busy round. The keys of the pump work with a click, and the number keys, Enter and the letter M of the keyboard work as well. The game has two blocks of ten orders, in an order drawn by the page. In one block the pump shows the mode in a small label. In the other it shows the mode in a coloured band, writes the unit next to the number and repeats the setting on its Start key.

Part D: Look-alike drugs and defences in layers

The game below takes place in the medication room of the ward. The wall cabinet holds eighteen bins, and six pairs of their drugs have names that look alike. Each order names one drug, and you pick its bin with a click before the time bar runs out after five seconds. Work as fast as a busy nurse at the start of a round.

The game has two blocks of twelve orders, in an order drawn by the page. In one block the labels are written in small letters, in alphabetical order, so that look-alike names stand side by side. In the other block the labels use tall man lettering, which writes the letters that differ in capitals. The look-alike names stand on different shelves, and a scanner checks every pick. The table counts your wrong picks, the wrong picks that the scanner stopped and those that would have reached the patient.

Part E: By hand: The evaluation of the clinic app

Five evaluators found sixteen problems in the clinic app. Their matrix of five rows and sixteen columns holds 34 ones, and six problems were found by exactly one evaluator. Compute the three values with a calculator, with natural logarithms. Round \(\lambda_{est}\), \(GT_{adj}\) and \(\lambda_{adj}\) to three decimals, the share to three decimals and the number of evaluators up to a whole number.
