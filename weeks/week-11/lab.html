Mobile and multimodal lab

Three parts of this interactive lab are games in three dimensions (3D). Part A hands the phone to ten patients in the waiting room. Part C sets up a tablet for gestures, and Part D lets you type on a phone, sitting and walking. Parts B and D compute the formulas of the lecture step by step, and Part E asks for the attendance of stage 11 by hand. In the calculators every intermediate value is rounded, and the next step continues with the rounded value. The results of Parts A, C and D can be copied into the Colab notebook. Each game first shows its room of the hospital. Start moves the view to the game, and the view returns to the room at the end. The games work best with a mouse or a touchpad on a computer.

Part A: One hand on the phone

Ten patients in the waiting room want to use the four main actions of the clinic app: Book, My visits, Call and Directions. Each one holds the phone in one hand, the right or the left, because the other hand is busy or because the patient is left-handed. The green band on the screen is the functional area of that patient's thumb in the model of the lecture, and the thumb tries the four buttons one after another.

In round 1, the patients try the layout of the current app: Call them one by one with Next patient or the key N. Then place the four buttons yourself in the grid below the view: Choose an action, then a cell. The readout shows at once the share of all patients of the clinic who would reach your buttons. Start round 2 with the same patients.

Part C: Gestures on the tablet

The check-in tablet in the waiting room accepts five gestures as shortcuts. A check confirms, a caret goes back to the top, a zigzag cancels, an arrow opens the next screen and a circle refreshes. The card at the top of the tablet shows the gesture to draw, with its first point as a dot and its direction as an arrow. Draw it on the tablet with the mouse, a touchpad or a finger, in one stroke.

The recognizer of the lecture scores each stroke against the five templates and names the best one, or answers no match below a score of 0.8. Round 1 asks for each gesture twice at a comfortable speed, and round 2 twice as fast as you can. Try one stroke the wrong way round to see what the recognizer does with it.

Part D: Typing in the waiting room

A patient writes short messages to the clinic on her phone. You take her place: Tap the keys of the phone in the view with the mouse, a touchpad or a finger. Your keyboard works as well, but each key press then lands where the key is at that moment. The phrase to type stands at the top of the screen. ⌫ deletes the last character, and done ends the phrase.

In round 1 the patient sits in the waiting room. In round 2 she walks down the corridor, and the phone moves in her hand with every step. Each round has five phrases. The time of a phrase runs from its first key to done, and every key counts, the corrections included.

Part E: By hand: Attendance with reminders

Without reminders, 0.82 of the patients of the clinic attend their appointments. Reminders by short message service multiply the odds of attending by 1.48. Compute the odds without reminders, the odds with reminders and the share who attend with reminders. Round each intermediate value to three decimals, as in the lecture.
