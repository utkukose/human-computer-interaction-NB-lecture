Prototypes and navigation lab

Three parts of this interactive lab are games in three dimensions (3D) that record your own actions. Part A lets you walk menu trees as signposts in the hospital. Part C asks you to sort cards at the meeting table, and Part D makes you the computer of a paper prototype. Parts B and D compute the formulas of the lecture step by step, and Part E asks for the values of a menu tree by hand. In the calculators every intermediate value is rounded, and the next step continues with the rounded value. The results of Parts A, C and D can be copied into the Colab notebook. Each game first shows its room of the hospital. Start moves the view to the game, and the view returns to the room at the end. The games work best with a mouse or a touchpad on a computer.

Part A: Signposts in the hospital

The game below takes place in the main hall of the hospital. A wayfinding board stands in front of eight doors. Each row of the board leads through a door to the next hall with its own board, just as a menu leads to the next menu. Your task is to find a department named at the top of the view. Click a row of the board to follow it, and use the row Back when a hall does not lead to your department.

The game has three trees with the same 64 departments, in an order drawn by the page. They are three levels of four rows, two levels of eight rows and one directory of all 64 names from A to Z. Each tree asks for four departments. The page times every walk from the first board to the right department and counts every wrong turn. The clock runs only while a board waits for your choice, so the walks from door to door do not count, just as the time model counts reading and choosing.

Part C: A card sort at the meeting table

The game below is an open card sort at the meeting table of the design team. Sixteen cards name services of the clinic app. Drag each card onto one of the five piles so that the cards in a pile belong together for you. There is no right answer: The sort records how you see the services. A card can be moved again at any time, and the name of each card appears at the bottom of the view when the pointer rests on it.

Name the piles in the fields below the scene, in your own words, and finish when all sixteen cards lie in at least two piles. The page then compares your piles with the five groups that a class of twenty participants formed.

Part D: A paper prototype in the usability lab

The game below takes place in the usability lab. A participant tests a paper prototype of the new booking flow, and you play the computer. Eight paper screens lie in front of you. After each tap of the participant, lay down the paper that the prototype shows next: Click it, and it slides to the participant. The rules of the prototype stand in the table below the scene, and a loupe in the corner of the view enlarges the paper under the pointer.

The session has eleven taps, among them a wrong turn of the participant and two steps back. A wrong paper surprises the participant and counts as an error, and you choose again. The page times every response from the moment of the tap.

Part E: By hand: A tree of four categories

The team also considers four categories with ten departments each. The categories are chosen right with \(s = 0.92\) and the departments with 0.98, and \(a = 1.2\) s and \(c = 0.11\) s as in the lecture. Compute the expected time of this tree to two decimals and the share of walks without a wrong turn to three decimals. Then compute the difference to the 4.07 s of stage 7.
