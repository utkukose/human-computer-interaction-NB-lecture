Pointing and touch lab

Three parts of this interactive lab are games in three dimensions (3D) that record your own movements. Part A repeats Fitts' law step by step and then asks you to tap rings of targets on a kiosk, the standard test of pointing. Part B repeats the probability of a hit step by step. Part C gives your pointer a tremor, and Part D is a buzz wire that measures steering. Part E asks for the values of the keypad for a personal identification number (PIN) by hand. In the calculators every intermediate value is rounded, and the next step continues with the rounded value. The results of Parts A, C and D can be copied into the Colab notebook. Each game first shows its room of the hospital. Start moves the view to the screen or the board, and the view returns to the room at the end. The games work best with a mouse or a touchpad on a computer.

Part A: Fitts' law and the ring of targets

The calculator repeats the worked example of Fitts' law one step at a time. Its sliders set the distance \(D\), the width \(W\) and the two constants \(a\) and \(b\). The figure places the current target on the line of the law.

The game below is the standard test of pointing, played at the check-in kiosk in the lobby of the hospital. The kiosk shows a ring of nine round targets, and one of them is orange. Tap or click it, and the next orange target appears on the far side of the ring. Work as fast as you can while still hitting most targets. The game works best with a mouse or a touchpad on a computer.

The game has six rings, with two diameters and three sizes of target, in an order drawn by the page. Each ring starts when you tap its first target, which is not timed, and ends after nine timed taps. The table gives your mean time for each ring, your misses, your effective width and your throughput. The section on effective width and throughput of the lecture page explains the last two.

Part C: Tremor at the kiosk

The kiosk of the hospital pharmacy below shows one green button at a time, from 6 to 20 mm wide. Move the pointer to the button and click or tap it. The round marker shows where your fingertip is, and the tap lands exactly there.

The game has two blocks of twenty buttons, in an order drawn by the page. In one block the pointer is steady. In the other it trembles with an amplitude of 4 mm at about five swings per second, like the hand of a patient with a tremor. Try to hit the buttons anyway: Waiting for a calm moment does not help much, because the tremor never stops. Part D: Buzz wire

The wooden board below stands in the play corner of the waiting hall. It is a buzz wire, the steering game of a fair. A tunnel runs between two metal rails from a green pad on the left to a chequered pad on the right. Move the pointer onto the green pad, then steer it through the tunnel to the chequered pad without touching a rail, as fast as you can. No click is needed. On a touch screen, drag a finger. Touching a rail sends you back to the green pad, and the restart is counted.

There are six tunnels, 120 or 240 mm long and 10, 20 or 40 mm wide, and each is run twice in an order drawn by the page. The time runs from leaving the green pad to reaching the chequered one. The table gives your mean time and your restarts for each tunnel.

Part E: By hand: The PIN pad of the payment kiosk

The payment kiosk of the hospital has a PIN pad with square keys 12 mm wide. The longest movement between two keys is 45 mm. Use \(a = 0.10\) s and \(b = 0.16\) s per bit. A patient with a steady spread of 3.0 mm has a tremor of amplitude 4 mm, and \(\Phi(1.46) = 0.9279\). Compute the three values with a calculator. Round the index of difficulty and the time to two decimals, the spread and \(z\) to two decimals and the probability to three decimals.
