Conversations and ethics lab

Three parts of this interactive lab are games in three dimensions (3D). Part A puts you at the kiosk of the entrance hall with the booking assistant. Part C hands you the tablet of the waiting room, and Part D makes you the supervisor of the clinic's chatbot at night. Parts B and D compute the formulas of the lecture step by step, and Part E asks for the values of stage 14 by hand. In the calculators every intermediate value is rounded, and the next step continues with the rounded value. The results of Parts A, C and D can be copied into the Colab notebook. Each game first shows its place in the clinic. Start moves the view to the game, and the view returns to the room at the end. The games work best with a mouse or a touchpad on a computer.

Part A: The booking assistant

The player books appointments at the kiosk of the entrance hall. A card below the screen shows the department, the day and the time of each booking, four bookings in each round. The booking assistant asks for one value at a time, and the player says it with the buttons below the view. The recognizer mishears some values, as a real one does in a busy hall.

In round 1, the assistant never asks back and books what it heard. In round 2, it asks back when it is less than 80 percent sure of what it heard. A booking is right when all three values match the card. The time runs from the first question to the booking.

Part C: Consent and cancellation

The player takes the tablet of the waiting room and does two tasks in each round. First, turn on the reminders of appointments, but share nothing for offers or with partners. Second, cancel the paid Priority Line. Tap the screen of the tablet, or use the buttons below the view, which list the controls of the current screen.

Round 1 uses the first version of the app, and round 2 the second. Each task records the taps, the time and the outcome. A task can be given up, and then the outcome is what the app keeps without a choice.

Part D: The night supervisor

The player supervises the chatbot of the clinic at night, in the control room. Twelve conversations come in each round, and each shows what a patient wrote and how the chatbot answered. Let the chatbot continue, or take over and answer the patient yourself. Four conversations of each round need a person.

In round 1, the screen shows the conversation alone. In round 2, the monitor on the right also shows how sure the chatbot is about the intent of the patient and flags urgent words. The time runs from the arrival of a conversation to your choice.

Part E: By hand: The time of a dark pattern

In the keystroke-level model of Week 6, a tap with a decision takes three operators. These are a mental preparation M of 1.35 s, a pointing P of 1.10 s and a press B of 0.20 s. Compute the predicted times of the dark cancellation in 7 taps and of the fair one in 2 taps. Then compute the time of refusing marketing on the dark consent screen in 4 taps, and the difference between the two cancellations. Give each value in seconds to two decimals.
