Interaction lab

Three parts of this interactive lab are games in three dimensions (3D) that record your own interaction. Part A races two kiosks of the clinic app against each other, with a questionnaire after each kiosk. Part B repeats the four usability measures of the lecture step by step. Part C is a corridor of doors with different handles, and Part D is a cooktop with three layouts of knobs. Part E repeats the formulas of a task with several steps, step by step. Part F simulates 200 patients on the booking flow of the clinic app with five design changes to switch on and off. Part G asks for the three formulas of a chain of steps by hand. In Parts B and E every intermediate value is rounded, and the next step continues with the rounded value, as in a calculation by hand. The results of Parts A, C and D can be copied into the Colab notebook.

Part A: The kiosk race

The lobby of the city hospital has two self-service kiosks that book appointments in the same database. Each round shows a booking task at the top of the scene: A department, a day and a time. Carry it out on the kiosk as fast and as accurately as you can; the clock starts with the round. Play three rounds on each kiosk, in the order that the page has drawn for you. After the third round on a kiosk, rate it with the ten statements that appear below the scene. These statements form the System Usability Scale (SUS), which the lecture explains in the section on usability. The page reveals at the end which kiosk was designed system-centred and which user-centred.

Click the buttons on the kiosk screen. The keyboard works as well: Tab moves between the buttons of the screen, Enter presses the marked button, the digit keys type on a keypad and Esc goes back. Look around the kiosk before you start, because a kiosk may hold information outside its screen. The table counts as an error every error message and every action that undoes an earlier one, such as Back or Esc.

Part B: Usability measures, step by step

The first calculator repeats the worked example of the lecture one step at a time. Its sliders set the number of participants \(n\), the successes \(s\), the mean time of the successful participants \(\bar{t}_s\) and the mean time of the participants who gave up \(\bar{t}_f\). The figure shows each participant as a dot and the total time as a bar.

The second calculator scores one SUS questionnaire. Its ten sliders hold the answers \(x_1\) to \(x_{10}\), from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). The figure shows the contribution of each statement.

Back to the lecture: Usability: Effectiveness, efficiency and satisfaction, where the text continues after the box of this part.

Part C: The corridor of doors

A corridor of the hospital has twelve doors. Each door opens either by pushing or by pulling, and only its hardware tells you which. Click the handle to pull, and click the door leaf or its plate to push. The keys ↑ (push) and ↓ (pull) work as well. Open every door as fast as you can. A wrong first try makes the door rattle, and you try again. The page records whether your first try was right, how long it took and how long the door took to open. The table names the four kinds of doors only after the run.

Part D: The cooktop in 3D

A cooktop with four burners lights one burner at a time in yellow. Click the knob that controls it as fast as you can. The keys 1 to 4 also choose the knobs, from left to right. For the square of knobs, 1 and 2 are the back row and 3 and 4 the front row. Run twelve trials with each of the three layouts, then compare them in the table. Drag the scene to look at it from another side; the view does not change the task.

Part E: A task as a chain of steps, step by step

The three steps of the worked example: Choosing the department (\(t_1 = 12\) s, \(r_1 = 10\) s), picking a date (\(t_2 = 10\) s, \(r_2 = 12\) s) and confirming (\(t_3 = 4\) s, \(r_3 = 25\) s). The sliders set the slip probabilities \(e_1, e_2, e_3\) and the share of slips that end with giving up, \(a\), which is the same for all three steps here. The figure stacks the time of each step and its expected repair time.
