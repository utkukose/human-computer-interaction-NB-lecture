Human-AI lab

Three parts of this interactive lab are games in three dimensions (3D). Part A puts you at the triage desk with an assistant that suggests a department. Part C takes you to the consulting room, where you predict the assistant, and Part D places you beside a delivery robot of the ward. Parts B and D compute the formulas of the lecture step by step, and Part E asks for the values of stage 13 by hand. In the calculators every intermediate value is rounded, and the next step continues with the rounded value. The results of Parts A, C and D can be copied into the Colab notebook. Each game first shows its place in the clinic. Start moves the view to the game, and the view returns to the room at the end. The games work best with a mouse or a touchpad on a computer.

Part A: The triage desk

The player is the triage nurse of the clinic. Twelve patients come to the desk in each round. The screen of the assistant shows the complaints on each form and the department that the assistant suggests. The assistant is right for about eight patients in ten. Asking the patient takes a moment, and the answer always points to the right department.

In round 1, the screen shows the suggestion alone. In round 2, it also shows the confidence of the assistant. For each patient, ask a question if you wish, by the button or by clicking the patient, and then choose a department. The time runs from the arrival of the patient at the desk to your choice.

Part C: What would the assistant say?

In the consulting room, a patient sits across the desk. The screen shows the form, the department that the assistant suggests and the runner-up. Then the patient corrects the form and removes one complaint. Predict which department the assistant will suggest now. Eight patients come in each round.

In round 1, the screen shows the suggestion, its confidence and the runner-up. In round 2, it also shows the contrastive explanation: The points that each feature adds to the lead of the suggestion over the runner-up, and the lead itself.

Part D: The delivery robot

At the nurse station of a ward, a sample must go to the laboratory at the end of the corridor, eighteen times in each round. Send it with the delivery robot or walk it yourself. The robot takes 2 minutes when it arrives. When it fails, the sample must be taken again, which costs 20 minutes more. Walking takes 8 minutes. The robot may change during a round.

In round 1, nothing tells you about the robot except what you see it do. In round 2, the display beside its dock shows how many of the last 20 deliveries of the hospital's robots arrived, and notices about updates.

Part E: By hand: Ten suggestions at the threshold

The assistant makes ten suggestions with the confidences 0.96, 0.92, 0.88, 0.82, 0.77, 0.74, 0.66, 0.61, 0.55 and 0.48. The suggestions at 0.82, 0.66 and 0.55 are wrong, and the other seven are right. The app shows a suggestion when its confidence is at least 0.8 and refers the other patients to a nurse who is right 0.9 of the time. Compute the coverage, the share right among the shown suggestions, the accuracy of the team and the expected calibration error with the shown and the other suggestions as two bins. Give the first three values to two decimals and the last to three significant digits.
