Usability testing lab

Three parts of this interactive lab are games in three dimensions (3D). Part A puts you behind the one-way mirror of the usability lab, where you code seven test sessions. Part C puts you at the recruiting desk at the entrance, and Part D lets you run six A/B tests at two kiosks. Parts B and D compute the formulas of the lecture step by step, and Part E asks for the intervals of stage 9 by hand. In the calculators every intermediate value is rounded, and the next step continues with the rounded value. The results of Parts A, C and D can be copied into the Colab notebook. Each game first shows its room of the hospital. Start moves the view to the game, and the view returns to the room at the end. The games work best with a mouse or a touchpad on a computer.

Part A: The observation room

The clinic runs a pilot test of the current app in its usability lab. A participant stands at the kiosk and thinks aloud, a moderator sits beside, and you watch from the observation room behind the one-way mirror. The monitor on your desk shows the screen of the kiosk. Seven sessions play one after another at three times their real speed, with a clock of the session time.

Code each session with the five keys below the view, or with the letters on your keyboard. Press S when the moderator says Please begin and E at every tap on a wrong option. At the end, press D for completed, H for completed with help or G for gave up. Help is any information from the moderator that moves the participant on, such as where to tap or whether a choice is right. A neutral question is no help.

Part C: The recruiting desk

The team needs eight participants for a test of the current app, and you recruit them at the main entrance of the hospital. People come in one at a time and stop at your desk. Their card shows the name and the age. Press Invite or Let pass below the view, or the keys I and P. Some people agree and walk to the usability lab, others have no time.

In round 1, fill the eight places as fast as you can. In round 2, fill quotas by age group like the patients of the clinic: Two people under 40, three aged 40 to 64 and three aged 65 and over. After each round, the board shows who completed the booking in the test.

Part D: Two kiosks, one winner?

Two kiosks in the lobby run an A/B test: Kiosk A shows the current design and kiosk B the redesign, and each patient uses one of them at random. After every ten patients per kiosk, the board above the kiosks shows the completions, the interval of each kiosk and the p-value of the N-1 test.

After each look, decide with the buttons below the view or with the keys. C continues, A stops because A is better, B stops because B is better, and N stops with no difference. A test ends after 100 patients per kiosk at the latest. After your decision, the board reveals the true completion rates of both kiosks. Six tests follow one another, and some of them have no real difference at all.

Part E: By hand: The intervals of stage 9

In stage 9, 8 of 13 patients completed the booking without help with the current app, and 11 of 13 with the redesign. Compute the adjusted Wald interval of each design with $z = 1.96$, so $z^2 = 3.84$ and $z^2/2 = 1.92$. Round each intermediate value to three decimals, as in the lecture.
