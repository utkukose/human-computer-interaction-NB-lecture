Perception lab

Three parts of this interactive lab are games in three dimensions (3D) that record your own perception. Part A calls queue codes on the display of the 3D waiting room and measures how small a code can become before you misread it. A calculator then repeats the visual angle step by step. Part B repeats the contrast ratio of the lecture step by step. Part C asks you to follow coloured lines on the floor of a 3D corridor, with typical colours and with simulated deuteranopia. Part D lets you rate and paint the walls of the 3D waiting room. Part E asks for three values of the audit by hand. In the calculators every intermediate value is rounded, and the next step continues with the rounded value, as in a calculation by hand. The results of Parts A, C and D can be copied into the Colab notebook.

Part A: Read the queue display

You sit in the waiting room of the clinic, and the display on the front wall calls queue codes such as K47: One letter and two digits. Each code appears at one of seven sizes, seen from one of four rows of chairs. Type the code that you see and press Enter. If you cannot read it, guess or press Cannot read it. There is no time limit, but answer as soon as you are sure. After 21 codes, the table shows how often you read each size correctly.

The page computes the visual angle of every code at your own eye. For that it needs the size of your screen and your distance from it, so set both first. Hold a bank card or an identity card flat against the screen and move the slider until the grey box has the same width as the card. Then enter the distance from your eyes to the screen, measured or estimated. Without these steps the page assumes a standard screen and 60 cm, and your angles are less exact.

Part B: Contrast, step by step

The calculator repeats the worked example of the lecture one step at a time. The two lists choose the text colour and the background colour among colours of the clinic app, and the presets load four pairs of the audit. Every channel value \(V\) is linearized, the two relative luminances \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) follow, and the contrast ratio decides the verdict. The figure shows the pair as it looks and places its ratio on the scale of the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG).

Part C: Follow the line

Four coloured lines run along the floor of a hospital corridor, cross each other and end at four doors. Each trial names one colour: Follow that line and choose the door where it ends, as fast and as accurately as you can. Click the door, or press 1 to 4 for the doors from left to right. A trial ends after ten seconds without an answer.

The game has three blocks of eight trials. The first block shows the colours as most people see them. The other two blocks show the corridor as a person with deuteranopia sees it, simulated with the matrix of the lecture. One of them uses colour alone, and the other adds symbols to the lines. The page draws the order of these two blocks for you. A sign on the left of the corridor names every line, as hospitals do.

Part D: Paint the waiting room

The waiting room of the clinic appears with its walls in sixteen colours, one after another, in an order drawn by the page. For each colour, imagine waiting here for your appointment and rate two feelings on scales from 1 to 9. The first asks how pleasant the room feels, from 1 (unpleasant) to 9 (pleasant). The second asks how calm or excited you feel in it, from 1 (calm) to 9 (excited). These are the dimensions of pleasure and arousal of the lecture. There are no right answers; rate your first impression.

After the sixteenth colour, the sliders below the scene let you paint the room in a colour of your own. While you paint, the page predicts your feelings from a small model fitted to your own sixteen ratings. It also shows the prediction of Valdez and Mehrabian and the contrast of the dark and the white text of the wall sign on your colour.

Part E: By hand: A pharmacy app

A pharmacy app prints its opening hours in grey #595959 on white, and the pharmacy hangs a sign with letters 25 mm high, read from 4 m. Its new wall colour has the brightness \(B = -0.5\) and the saturation \(S = 1.0\) in standard units. Compute the three values with a calculator. Round intermediate values to four decimals, the ratio to two decimals, the angle to one decimal and the pleasure to three decimals.
