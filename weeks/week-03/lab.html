Attention and memory lab

Three parts of this interactive lab are games in three dimensions (3D) that record your own attention, decisions and memory. Part A asks you to find an icon on the waiting-room display, in feature and in conjunction search. Part B repeats the Hick-Hyman law and the information of uneven choices step by step. Part C rebuilds Hick's experiment as a desk of lamp buttons, and Part D tests how long a code you can hold between two screens of the kiosk. Part E asks for the times of a menu by hand. In the calculators every intermediate value is rounded, and the next step continues with the rounded value. The results of Parts A, C and D can be copied into the Colab notebook.

Part A: Find the icon

The display of the waiting room shows a grid of icons. Look for one target: A red circle. Press J or Present if it is there and F or Absent if it is not, as fast as you can without mistakes. Before each display a small cross appears in the middle; keep your eyes on it.

The game has two blocks of sixteen displays, with 4, 8, 16 or 32 icons. In the block of feature search all other icons are blue circles, so colour alone finds the target. In the block of conjunction search the other icons are blue circles and red squares, so the target shares its colour with some icons and its shape with others. The page draws the order of the blocks for you. After both blocks, the table compares your mean times.

Part B: The Hick-Hyman law, step by step

The first calculator repeats the worked example of the lecture one step at a time. Its sliders set the number of options \(n\), the base time \(a\) and the time per bit \(b\). The figure places the current choice on the curve of the law.

The second calculator computes the information \(H\) of a choice whose options are not equally likely, for four distributions of shares, and the expected choice time \(a + bH\).

Back to the lecture: Choosing among options: The Hick-Hyman law, where the text continues after the box of this part.

Part C: Hick lamps

A control desk holds eight round buttons with lamps, numbered 1 to 8. In each block only some of them are in play: 1, 2, 4 or all 8. After a short, random wait one lamp lights up. Press its button as fast as you can, with the number key on the keyboard or with a click. A wrong button counts as an error, and the next trial follows.

The game has four blocks, one for each number of lamps, in an order drawn by the page. Each block starts with two practice trials and continues with ten trials. Keep the fingers of one hand on the number keys of the lamps in play. After all four blocks, the table gives your mean time for each number of lamps and fits the Hick-Hyman law to them.

Part D: Remember the code

The kiosk of the lobby shows a booking code for a short time, then asks for it after a pause of three seconds. Type the code on the keypad of the kiosk or with the digit keys, and confirm with OK or Enter. Backspace deletes the last digit. Do not write the code down: The game measures what your working memory holds.

There are fourteen codes, from 4 to 10 digits. Half of them appear as one row of digits, the other half in groups of three, such as 471 820 39. The page draws the order. After the fourteenth code, the table shows which codes you recalled for each length and format.

Part E: By hand: A pharmacy menu

A pharmacy app lists 25 medicines in one alphabetical list. Reading a name takes \(c = 0.25\) s, with \(a = 0.40\) s for search. For decisions, \(a = 0.20\) s and \(b = 0.15\) s per bit, and pointing takes \(T_p = 1.0\) s. Compute the three values with a calculator. Round the information to two decimals, the times to three decimals and the last value to two decimals.
